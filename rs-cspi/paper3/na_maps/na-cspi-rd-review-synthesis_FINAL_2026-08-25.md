# North America CSPI and RD raster effort, reviewed for finalization: synthesis

*25 August 2026. A same-session read of the full na_maps record, anchored on the 19 August blocker review, its working record, the 19 August red team pass, the component-count reconciliation memo, the RD ceiling decision (12 August), the RD units rebuild report (11 August), and the Zenodo correction package (draft). Written to answer one question, namely what remains before this effort can be called finished, with the ESI-across-Canada premise checked against the record rather than assumed.*

## The ESI-Canada premise needs correcting, not just answering

ESI across Canada already exists at coarse grain. `canada_nfi/NorthAmerica_ESI.tif` and `Acadian_ESI.tif` (26 to 30 June) are on disk, trained on roughly 16,000 Canadian CAN and BC plots already present in the North America site-tree compilation, and validated with a proper transfer test rather than blind extrapolation. That test is the right cautionary note to carry forward as it showed a CONUS-trained model fails badly north of the border (R² 0.122 overall, negative above 55 N) while a model fit on the joint CONUS-plus-Canada compilation predicts Canadian plots well (within-Canada R² 0.850, RMSE 5.1 m). So the coarse ESI is not the gap.

The real gap is the 30 m downscale, and it is not open so much as contested. The rescope memo retired ESI30 as a terrain-plus-soils downscale (adds only 0.004 skill over climate alone, judged fabricated resolution) and left one live route, a direct plot-to-embedding model gated on within-block explained-variance share above 20 percent. That gate ran on 19 August (job 13728889) and returned a share of 0.1119, below threshold, so the memo declares ESI30 closed. The same-day red team flags this verdict as not yet trustworthy (finding A4), since the job decomposed the sum-of-squares share of the raw response variables rather than the registered share of *explained* variance from a fitted model, and since the response column's identity (a 1 km climate-model prediction with a 0.981 true-versus-fuzzed correlation) makes a low within-block share close to guaranteed regardless of whether site index is mappable at 30 m at all. Until that gate is rerun as specified, "ESI30 is closed" and "ESI30 is open" are both premature. This is the first item that needs doing, not because the answer is likely to flip, but because the current answer was reached with the wrong instrument.

## What is actually shippable now

Of the four requested NA 30 m layers (CSPI, BGI, ESI, RD), RD is the only one with a live path, and it is CONUS-first. The per-tile calibration finished 14 August (24,544 tiles, 6.7 GB), and CONUS assembly into the two decided products, a calibrated layer and a quantile-mapped display layer, was running as job 13728908 as of 19 August after a first attempt failed on a miscalibrated lookup table. BGI30 is not admitted (blocked R² 0.159 against the pre-registered gate, verdict EQUIVOCAL rather than the "failed" the blocker memo states, per red team finding B). The full three-component composite does not exist at NA extent and cannot yet, since the honest four-way NA intersection is 2,220 cells, Canadian Asym is quarantined for reproducing standing biomass at r = 1.000, Canadian BGI is barred from any composite, and no continental soils raster exists.

The RD30 assembly itself is not safe to treat as final once it lands. The red team's strongest finding (A1) is that every pre-registered gate on that job checks the input plot table, the file count, or that the mosaic faithfully reproduces its own tiles, none of which would catch a repeat of the exact defect class that created this entire correction chain, namely a constant multiplicative error baked into the tiles before assembly. A cheap fix exists, sampling the assembled mosaic at plot-adjacent cells and checking it against the out-of-fold calibrated distribution, and it is not yet in the gate set. A second finding (C1) is that the quantile-mapped display product's validation covered a different transform (plot predictions to plot field values) than the one being deployed (map pixels to plot field values), so its metadata needs a stated restriction against subnational distributional claims before anyone downstream computes a regional RD summary from it.

## Canada-side RD is blocked on data, not compute, in three combined ways

AlphaEarth embeddings do not exist for British Columbia, Yukon, Northwest Territories, or Nunavut, so any 30 m Canadian raster today is a nine-province product at best. A turnkey Earth Engine launcher is built and waiting, since Claude's tools cannot fire Earth Engine exports directly, and the red team's one addition worth making before the expensive BC run (D1) is a single overlap-granule diff against an already-staged tile to rule out silent embedding-model drift. Ground alignment is separately unfinished, as 12,920 embedded Canadian plots, all of Alberta, Ontario, and Prince Edward Island plus partial coverage elsewhere, have no rows in the remeasurement-aligned table, so the usable intersection for fitting anything is 13,320 plots in three provinces. And the RD-to-SDI conversion has no Canadian denominator, since 64.8 percent of Canadian sites fall through the ecoregion lookup to a single CONUS forest-type-group median (100 percent in taiga and tundra), and RD itself fails transport across the border (leave-one-country-out NSE -2.865 Canada into CONUS). None of the three closes with more Cardinal allocation.

## Record hygiene before anything ships

Three items sit between the current state and a defensible release. First, the CSPI component-count contradiction is now reconciled on paper, namely CSPI v2 of record is the three-component composite (ESI plus BGI plus Asym) per the manuscript, the composite specification, and the verified build log, while the five-component raster on disk is a genuine but experimental release-engineering artifact that six files still describe as the definition. The reconciliation memo lists the exact dated-banner edit each of those six files needs, and none has been applied yet. Second, the Zenodo v1.4.0 correction package for the defective RD CSV (identical error across four published versions, 336 downloads) is drafted but not published, and carries one must-fix of its own, namely the paste-ready description states a corrected median that is true of the RD_uncap convention and false of the file the run sheet actually ships, which would publish a correction that contradicts its own data on the first number a reader checks. Third, the F1 jurisdiction sweep run this session covers bounding-box extent only, catching 14 name-versus-extent mismatches, but the failure mode that motivated the check in the first place, the NLCD and New Brunswick defect, was healthy-looking extent with a dead or wrong interior, and that point-lattice probe has still not been run over the NA chain. Separately, the blocker memo's own scope claim has a hole, since a review titled North America at 30 m never mentions that the Alaska window is declared but unoccupied (the composite stops at 141 W), which belongs in the record alongside the four missing Canadian territories.

## Where this leaves the effort

The project has already done the hard part of finding these problems, largely through its own adversarial review discipline, and the remaining work is enumerable rather than open-ended. Nothing here requires new science. It requires rerunning one gate correctly, adding two gate checks to a job that is otherwise ready to assemble, applying six banner edits already specified word for word, fixing one sentence in a metadata draft, running one additional probe, and then the Canada-side data acquisition (embeddings, alignment, a Canadian density denominator) that was always going to be the long pole and was never a compute problem to begin with.
