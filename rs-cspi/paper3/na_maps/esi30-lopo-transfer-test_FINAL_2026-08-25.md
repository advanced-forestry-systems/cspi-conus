## Headline verdict

The ESI30 embeddings model does not reproduce the BGI30 failure pattern. Held out entirely from training and predicted from a model fit on the other province alone, Quebec returns a leave-one-province-out predictive R² of 0.3110 with a calibration slope of 1.078, and New Brunswick returns R² of 0.1960 with a slope of 1.054. Pooled across both held-out folds, the transfer R² is 0.2852 with a slope of 1.094 on n = 18,454, against a blocked five-fold R² of 0.4891 from today's earlier gate rerun. That is a real degradation under jurisdictional transfer, roughly a 42 percent relative drop in explained variance, but every number stays positive and well calibrated. Applying the project's own three-tier admission bar (R² at least 0.25 and slope at least 0.50 for PASS, R² below 0.15 for a clear NEGATIVE, anything else EQUIVOCAL) to each fold separately, Quebec held out is a clean PASS, New Brunswick held out is EQUIVOCAL rather than negative, and the pooled transfer result is itself a PASS on the identical bar the blocked design cleared. This is the opposite of what BGI30 showed under the same design: BGI30's blocked result was itself only equivocal (R² 0.159) and its leave-one-province-out transfer collapsed to a pooled R² of −0.839, with two of three held-out provinces scoring negative. ESI30's transfer skill is weaker than its within-domain skill, as transfer always is, but it survives the province boundary, which is the specific question this test exists to answer. One caveat governs the whole result and is stated plainly in the unknowns ledger below: the aligned table's usable topht plus embedding rows cover exactly two provinces, Quebec and New Brunswick, so this is a two-fold transfer test between two Canadian jurisdictions, not a test against a province the model has never seen data resemble at all.

## Per-province result table

Model: ranger random forest, 500 trees, seed 42, predicting topht (measured top or dominant height, m) from the 64 AlphaEarth embedding bands (A01 through A64), identical specification to `esi30_gate_rerun.R` (job 13958776). Input tables: `nfi_truecoord_aligned_table.csv` joined to `aef_embeddings_by_site_2023.csv` on `magp_site_id`, joined n = 21,788, usable n (topht non-missing) = 18,454, matching today's earlier gate rerun exactly. The grouping field is `province` from the aligned table (`province.x` after the merge), the same field BGI30 used for its own leave-one-province-out design. Only two provinces carry usable topht plus embedding pairs at this join, Quebec (11,520 plots, 62.4 percent) and New Brunswick (6,934 plots, 37.6 percent), so the leave-one-province-out design here has exactly two folds rather than BGI30's three. Bias is mean(predicted − observed), so both folds slightly over-predict on average, New Brunswick more so. New Brunswick, the smaller province and the one being predicted from a model trained entirely on Quebec, is the weaker fold, consistent with the general pattern in this project that the majority-share province transfers better than the minority one, but weaker here means equivocal, not the sharply negative results BGI30 returned for its minority provinces.

## Comparison against project precedent

Three prior transfer results in this project bear on how to read this one, and none of them used an identical model or response, so the comparison is directional rather than a strict apples-to-apples replication.

BGI30 (`BGI30_FIT_RESULT_2026-07-29.md`), the closest methodological precedent, used the identical admission bar and an identical blocked-versus-leave-one-province-out design on the same AlphaEarth embedding source, predicting net above-ground biomass increment (a multi-decadal growth rate) instead of topht (a directly observable stand attribute). Its blocked R² was 0.159, already only equivocal, and its pooled leave-one-province-out R² was −0.839, with Quebec-out at −1.056 and New Brunswick-out at −0.354, both negative, only Newfoundland and Labrador transferring at R² 0.217. ESI30's blocked result (0.489) is already three times stronger than BGI30's, and its transfer result stays positive in both folds where BGI30's went negative in two of three. Table 8 of the BGI30 report also found 42.2 percent of leave-one-province-out predictions falling outside the per-band training bounding box, a genuine extrapolation problem this task did not re-measure for ESI30 and flags below as an open item.

The June cross-border ESI transfer test (`CANADA_esi_transfer.csv`, `CANADA_esi_transfer_byband.csv`) is a different model entirely, the underlying ClimateNA ranger site-index model trained on the joint CONUS and Canada compilation, evaluated by training on CONUS alone and predicting into Canada, a genuinely unseen country rather than a held-out province within an already-Canadian panel. That test degraded far more severely, from a within-Canada five-fold ceiling of R² 0.850 to a CONUS-to-Canada transfer R² of 0.122, an 86 percent relative loss, and its by-band breakdown turns negative for the two largest site-index bands (55 to 60: R² −2.14; 60+: R² −7.19). ESI30's within-province-of-Canada transfer here (0.489 to 0.285, a 42 percent relative loss) is milder, but the two tests are not measuring the same kind of jurisdictional distance: June's test crossed a national border and an entirely disjoint climate-normal training domain, while today's test crosses a provincial boundary within the same country and the same embedding source. A true out-of-country test of the embeddings model, comparable in severity to the June ESI test, has not been run and should not be inferred from this result.

## Implementation notes

Methodology was taken directly from two source scripts rather than re-derived: `esi30_gate_rerun.R` (Cardinal, `aef_analysis/`, job 13958776) for the model specification, response, input tables, and join, and `bgi30_fit.R` (`na_maps/`) for the leave-one-province-out fold design and the `metric_block()` metric definitions (bias, MAE, RMSE, predictive R², squared Pearson correlation, observed-on-predicted slope), so today's transfer numbers are computed identically to both precedents and are directly comparable to each. One difference from BGI30's design is unavoidable rather than a deviation: BGI30 grouped its blocked folds on the province-and-block pair because `sblock` collided across its eight-province embeddings table, but leave-one-province-out folds by construction group on province regardless of block-label collisions, so this was a non-issue here.

The Cardinal connector reproduced the same instability documented in today's earlier gate-rerun report, repeated "Connection closed" errors on `cardinal_run`, in streaks of two to eight before self-recovering, and one `cardinal_submit_job` call that silently failed to submit (confirmed absent from `squeue` afterward) with no error surfaced beyond the same connection message. Rather than escalate or raise the timeout, every failing call was reissued per the standing convention, and after two failed `cardinal_submit_job` attempts the job was run directly on the login node under `setsid nohup ... &` with output polled from a log file, which the compute's own runtime (well under a minute for two ranger fits on 18,454 rows) made appropriate. Writing the roughly 100-line R script over the connector again required chunked heredoc appends rather than one write, matching the workaround already recorded in the earlier gate-rerun report; one chunk in the middle returned "Connection closed" to the client but had in fact landed on disk, confirmed by re-reading the file's line count immediately after, so a failed-looking write was reissued once unnecessarily before that was noticed.

No FIA or NFI true coordinates were read, written, or displayed at any point. Both input tables were re-verified for the absence of any coordinate-named column before the join, matching the assertion in `esi30_gate_rerun.R`, and the check is re-run and printed in this task's own script log. All outputs are written under dated names (`esi30_lopo_by_province_20260825.csv`, `esi30_lopo_pooled_20260825.csv`, `esi30_lopo_oof_predictions_20260825.csv`, `esi30_lopo_province_composition_20260825.csv`, `esi30_lopo_transfer_comparison_20260825.csv`) in `aef_analysis/` on Cardinal scratch and contain only `magp_site_id`, province, observed, and predicted values, no coordinates. No file was deleted on Cardinal in the course of this task.
