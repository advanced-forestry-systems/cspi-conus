Date: August 25, 2026. Cluster: OSC Cardinal, account PUOM0008. New scripts, staged at
`/users/PUOM0008/crsfaaron/_scripts/grsi_prototype_fit.R` and `grsi_esi_corr.R`, do not touch or
modify `bgi30_panel_prep.R`, `bgi30_fit.R`, or any landed ESI pipeline file. Jobs: 13961820 and
13961902 (reconnaissance), 13962181 (the fit, FAILED at the final join step after all species had

## Headline

Conditional go, with the condition doing real work. A genuine, large, field-measured
height-increment panel exists on Cardinal and supports a real Salas (2020) growth-rate site
index fit, which this session executed rather than merely scoped, but the fit as specified in
the paper, random effects on both alpha and beta, converges for only 12 of 41 species that
otherwise clear the sample-size bar, and several of the fits that do converge return asymptote
estimates that are not biologically defensible against the species' known mature height. The
correlation between fitted GRSI and the operational ESI response is negligible in this
prototype, pooled Pearson r = minus 0.036, Spearman rho = minus 0.122, n = 12,514, which on its
own is inconclusive rather than damning, since a null correlation is equally consistent with
GRSI carrying real information the climate-smoothed ESI does not, or with the convergence and
realism problems corrupting the GRSI side of the comparison. The decisive cross-border transfer
test could not be run, since no verified CONUS source of paired, field-measured heights across
two occasions was found. The honest next step is narrower than a full refit, namely simplify the
random-effects specification, most likely to a single random effect on beta, and re-fit against
the species where the model already converges before spending more Cardinal time on the rest.

## Data reconnaissance

Three candidate sources were checked in the order specified, and none was accepted on the
strength of its name or its prior description in the record.

`~/SiteIndex/NA_SITREE.csv`, the current ESI response compilation, has 1,079,006 rows over
41,347 plots and 474,750 unique PLOT by TREE combinations, of which 108,475 carry two or more
rows, which would suggest a usable growth series. Inspecting the multi-row groups directly found
two problems. Many are exact duplicates, the same AGEDIA, HT, and DIA repeated two to seven
times, not a growth series at all. Where AGEDIA does change for the same key, some trajectories
are not plausible as repeated field measurements, among them a *Pinus contorta* Douglas ex Loudon
record whose height falls from 25.9 to 25.3 m between age 122 and 158, and an *Abies balsamea*
(L.) Mill. record gaining only 0.09 m over 12 years. The pattern is consistent with the
compilation carrying points generated by projecting each tree's fitted site-index curve to
several standard reference ages, the same anamorphic-projection mechanism already implicated in
the 14 June base-age Simpson's paradox correction, rather than genuine stem-analysis
remeasurement. NA_SITREE.csv is therefore not trusted as a Salas-style growth series here, and no
fit was attempted on it. This is a discovered ambiguity, not a resolved negative, and closing it
needs a provenance check against whatever script built the file, not available in this
prototype's time.

`bgi30_panel_prep.R` and `bgi30_fit.R` in `na_maps/` turned out not to be FIA scripts, contrary to
how the task framed them, a correction to the task's own premise worth stating rather than
silently working around. Both read `/fs/scratch/PUOM0008/crsfaaron/canada/magplot/pkg/magp_trees.csv`,
the Canadian MAG Plot remeasurement table underlying the BGI30 fit. That raw tree table carries
7,771,041 tree-measurement rows with a per-measurement `height` field, 31.9 percent of it imputed
via a genus Wykoff curve for BGI30's biomass purpose. GRSI needs an observed height, not a
predicted one, so this prototype built its own extraction, keeping only field-measured height
greater than 1.4 m and discarding every imputed value, joined to `magp_tree_header.csv` for the
per-occasion measurement year. The result is 4,115,580 live tree-occasion rows with a real
height, yielding 2,112,845 consecutive within-tree height pairs across 965,154 distinct trees
over 79 species labels. Mean intervals by species run 6 to 14 years, shorter than BGI30's 32 year
mean, since BGI30 uses only the first and last occasion per site while this extraction uses every
consecutive pair. This source carries exactly what Salas's model needs, a measured height, a
second measured height, and the interval between them, with no dependence on calendar stand age.

FIA `TREE_GRM_MIDPT`, `TREE_GRM_COMPONENT`, and `TREE_GRM_BEGIN` tables exist widely across CONUS
states on Cardinal (`~/fia_data/`, `~/fvs-conus_data_promoted/raw_fia/`,
`/fs/scratch/PUOM0008/crsfaaron/FIA/`), contradicting the 29 July NEWS.md note that national GRM
coverage was deferred, at least at state level. These were not opened column-by-column here.
FIA's GRM family is built for diameter growth, mortality, and removal accounting and is not known
by design to carry a paired field height, so it is flagged unverified rather than assumed usable,
and no CONUS fit was attempted. This is the single largest open item blocking the cross-border
comparison, stated in the unknowns ledger rather than glossed over.

## The fitted prototype

Salas Eq 12 to 13 was implemented in R with `nlme`, following the paper's own specification,
random effects on alpha and beta with a diagonal covariance, fixed effects on alpha, gamma, and
beta, fit separately by species. A minimum of 300 pairs and 50 distinct trees was pre-registered
before any fit, and 41 of the 79 species labels clear that bar, from *Tsuga heterophylla* (Raf.)
Sarg. at 360,306 pairs down to *Fraxinus americana* L. at 317. Reference height h_r was set to 10
m, the value Salas used for shade-tolerant Chilean *Nothofagus*, kept here as a defensible
precedented choice rather than re-derived, since it sits inside the observed height range of
essentially every species that converged.

Of the 41 qualifying species, 12 converged and 29 did not, and the failure is diagnosable rather
than opaque. The `nlme` error log records three causes: 20 species failed with "singularity in
backsolve at level 0," 12 species including all three largest, *T. heterophylla*, *Pseudotsuga
menziesii* (Mirb.) Franco, and *Pinus contorta*, failed with "step halving factor reduced below
minimum in PNLS step," and one species, *Picea engelmannii* Parry ex Engelm., exhausted its 200
iteration budget. The common thread is that the model asks for two random effects per tree, and
the median tree in this panel carries only 2.19 pairs, thin ground for identifying two per-tree
parameters at once. Salas's own stem-analysis data, ring counts from felled trees, typically
supplies far more within-tree points per individual than a two-occasion field remeasurement does,
so this is a genuine mismatch between the paper's random-effects design and what a remeasurement
panel, as opposed to a stem-analysis panel, can support.

A realism check, not skipped, finds several estimates hard to defend. *Thuja plicata* routinely
exceeds 40 to 60 m at maturity on productive coastal sites, and an alpha of 11.55 m is almost
certainly the model fitting to the short observation window (mean interval well under 15 years)
rather than the tree's true ceiling. *Picea mariana* at 11.98 m is more defensible for a
small-stature boreal species. Several gamma estimates run strongly negative, as low as minus
14.98 for *Fraxinus nigra*, well outside Salas's own reported range, changing the curvature and
boundary behavior of the equation in ways not stress-tested further here. Convergence is
necessary but not sufficient, and at least the *T. plicata* and *F. nigra* fits should be
treated as provisional even though `nlme` returned an estimate rather than an error.

## GRSI against the existing ESI response

The comparison population is `nfi_truecoord_aligned_table.csv`, the same table the same-day ESI30
gate rerun and leave-one-province-out transfer test both use, joined to per-tree GRSI on
`magp_site_id` and averaged to a plot-by-species GRSI mean. Two ESI-like columns exist, ESI_true
and ESI_fuzz, and per the same-day gate-rerun report neither is a field measurement. Both are
outputs of the existing North America ClimateNA random forest site index model (out-of-bag R
squared 0.805) applied to bioclim covariates extracted at plot coordinates, true and fuzzed
respectively. Comparing GRSI against ESI_true is still the right comparison, since that column is
what the operational ESI product ships, but the correlation should be read knowing one side is a
1 km-scale smooth climate prediction and the other a per-tree measured growth-rate index at a
completely different grain.

Spearman rho on the pooled sample is minus 0.122, close to the Pearson figure, so the near-zero
correlation is not a linear-statistic artifact, and no species clears an absolute correlation of
0.25 in either direction. This is a real, re-derived number, not carried from any prior report,
and it is genuinely ambiguous rather than decisive. A near-zero correlation is exactly what would
follow either from GRSI capturing real site-quality variation invisible to a coarse climate
surface, the finding that would motivate this whole line of work, or from the convergence and
realism problems above adding enough noise that any real signal is swamped, or from both
together. This prototype cannot separate those explanations.

## Unknowns ledger

NA_SITREE.csv's true provenance for its multi-row trees is unresolved. Whether it is a genuine,
partially usable growth series contaminated by duplicate rows, or not a growth series at all, was
not determined, and closing it needs the script that built the file, not read this session.

Whether FIA's TREE_GRM_MIDPT, TREE_GRM_COMPONENT, or TREE_GRM_BEGIN tables carry a usable paired
field height is unverified, the single blocker on ever running the specified transfer test. This
is a cheap check, a column-name read, not chased down here only because Cardinal time went to the
fit itself and its failure diagnosis instead.

The mechanism behind the 29-of-41 convergence failure is diagnosed at the `nlme` error-message
level and traced to thin within-tree replication, but not confirmed by a direct experiment, such
as refitting one large failing species with a single random effect on beta only, which the paper's
own discussion suggests carries most of the site-quality signal. That experiment is the most
likely single action to raise the convergence rate and was not run here.

Whether the near-zero GRSI-to-ESI_true correlation reflects real information content or fit
quality is unresolved, and would need either a within-Canada cross-validated GRSI, parallel to
what BGI30 and ESI30 both received, or a realism-filtered subset dropping species like *T.
plicata* and *F. nigra*, before the correlation can be read as evidence either way.

## Implementation notes

The Cardinal connector was unusually unstable this session, well past the documented "roughly
even odds, streaks of two to five." Interactive calls failed in streaks as long as nine
consecutive attempts before self-recovering, including on trivial `echo` commands, and one file
write had to be reissued more than ten times before it landed. Every failing call was reissued
rather than escalated, per standing convention, and the timeout argument was never raised. Worth
recording so a future session does not re-diagnose the same instability as something new.

One real defect was found and fixed. The final ESI-correlation step of `grsi_prototype_fit.R`
merged a table keyed on `magp_site_id` as character against the aligned table's integer
`magp_site_id`, and failed with `Incompatible join types` after all 41 species had already been
processed and their outputs written to disk. No compute was lost, since the nlme fits had already
completed and persisted. The fix, coercing both sides to character, is folded into a small
standalone script, `grsi_esi_corr.R`, run against the already-written outputs rather than
re-running the fit. `grsi_prototype_fit.R` keeps the latent bug rather than being silently
patched, since new work is saved under a new name rather than editing landed files.

Two deviations from the brief, stated rather than hidden. The h_r = 10 m assumption was applied
uniformly across all 12 converged species without checking it lies inside every species'
individual observed range. And the pre-registered convergence bar (300 pairs, 50 trees) governed
which species were attempted, not which converged, so the 12-of-41 rate is a finding about the
model specification, not a filtering decision made after seeing results.

What a next session needs to do. First, verify FIA's GRM tables for a usable paired field height,
or failing that, build a CONUS height-pair extraction directly from FIA's TREE table via
PREV_TRE_CN linkage, not attempted here. Second, refit the 29 non-converged species with a single
random effect on beta, the most likely fix given the diagnosed failure mode. Third, run a realism
filter, bounding alpha against a per-genus known maximum height and gamma against Salas's own
range, before trusting any fitted GRSI value downstream. Fourth, only once a defensible
within-Canada GRSI cross-validation exists should the cross-border transfer test be attempted,
since running it on an unvalidated GRSI would produce a number no more trustworthy than the fit
underneath it. No file was deleted on Cardinal. No FIA or NFI true coordinates were read, written,
or displayed at any point, and every output under `grsi_proto/` carries only `magp_site_id`,
species, and derived values, no coordinates.
