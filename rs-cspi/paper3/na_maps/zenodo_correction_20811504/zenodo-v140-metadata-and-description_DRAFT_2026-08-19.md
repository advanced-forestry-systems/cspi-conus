**ERRATUM, 6 August 2026. The relative density response published in every version of this record before 1.4.0 is wrong by a constant factor of 2.471. Every value in `conus_rd_uncapped_by_pid.csv` was 2.471 times too small, and the defect propagates into the small area estimation products, the prediction intervals, the trained model fit and all three 30 m rasters. If you hold a copy from versions 1.0.0 to 1.3.0, multiply the `RD` column by 2.471 to recover the intended quantity, and read the full erratum file `ERRATUM_2026-08-06.md` in this version before using any other file.**

The file was built as an Imperial additive stand density index numerator, `SDI_T1`, in trees per acre against a 10 inch reference diameter, divided by a metric maximum stand density index denominator, `SDImax.mean`, in trees per hectare. That mixed ratio drops the acres to hectares conversion of 2.471. The ratio of correct to published value is 2.471000 with a standard deviation of 4.63 x 10^-15 across all 123,173 plots, and no row deviates by more than 10^-9. The defect is an exact global scalar and a single multiplication undoes it for the response file.

The consequences for the published distribution are large, on the RD_uncap comparator scale, meaning the published response multiplied by the 2.471 factor derived above and measured over the same 123,173 plots as the published file. On that comparator scale the median relative density is 0.2821 as published and 0.6970 corrected, the mean is 0.3814 as published and 0.9424 corrected, and the share of plots with relative density above one, meaning stands denser than their own maximum density reference, is 4.511 percent as published and 36.410 percent corrected. Readers who took the published distribution as evidence that the response was well behaved were reading an artefact. **This comparator scale is not the file this version ships.** The response released in this version is the harmonised rebuild in `conus_rd_rebuilt_uncapped.csv` (299,193 plots, governed by `CONUS_RD_BUILD_2026-08-04.md`), whose own median is 0.428 uncapped, a different number from 0.6970 above because it corrects the diameter reference and rebuild population as well as the 2.471 units factor. Do not quote 0.6970 as the median of the shipped file. [UNVERIFIED at draft time: the exact median of the rebuilt file restricted to the same 123,173-plot comparison population has not been separately measured; re-derive it from the staged file before this description is pasted, per Stage A of the run sheet.]

The defective file is present, byte identical (md5 39322b45207a1996ef027bdde0491652), in all four prior versions: 1.0.0 (10.5281/zenodo.20811505), 1.1.0 (10.5281/zenodo.20816550), 1.2.0 (10.5281/zenodo.20981847) and 1.3.0 (10.5281/zenodo.20981913). It was never removed and never replaced. Zenodo does not delete files from a published version, so those files remain downloadable at their version DOIs permanently, and this notice is the only mechanism that reaches anyone who already holds a copy.

**How far it reaches.** Twenty files across the record are defective in value and are replaced or withdrawn in this version: the response itself, the prediction interval table, all eight small area estimation products from county through the 30 km hexagon, all three 30 m rasters including both small area calibrated surfaces, the saved model fit, and three figures. Sixteen more report at least one affected number alongside invariant ones and carry column level corrections. Thirty one files are unaffected. The small area chain is defective because `build_sae_product.R` takes its direct estimate from `conus_rsrd_oof.csv` column `RD_field`, which is the defective response under another name, verified to have the same distribution to the last measured digit.

**What is unaffected.** Correcting a response by a constant positive factor leaves scale free statistics unchanged, provided the estimator is scale equivariant and the model is refit. All Pearson and Spearman correlations computed on a fixed sample stand. All random forest coefficients of determination stand, including the headline cross validated skill of about 0.18, the spatial block value of 0.177, the residual Moran's I of 0.615 and every entry of `stress_test_results.csv`. The calibration slope of 1.0077 stands. Both disturbance dual role tables and the multinomial agent table stand, their predictors being standardised before fitting. Every small area variance reduction figure stands, including the 45 percent at county scale, the 68 percent at the 6 km hexagon and 10 km subcounty scales, the 41 percent against 29 percent auxiliary contrast, the Fay Herriot against unit level correlation of 0.99 and the interval coverage of 0.904, because all are ratios in which both terms scale together. The record's central comparative claim, that this surface tracks observed plot relative density better than the TreeMap 2022 product with Pearson coefficients of about 0.24 against 0.006 nationally and about 0.51 against 0.14 at the survey unit level, is scale free and stands.

**One exception to the invariance, which matters.** The multi objective and single objective support vector regression results are not protected. `mosvr.R` fits `e1071::svm` with `scale = FALSE` and searches a fixed absolute hyperparameter grid, so the estimator is not scale equivariant in the response and a refit on the corrected scale selects a different model. The four support vector rows in `postw_full_skill.csv` and `postw_vs_rank1_skill.csv` are recomputed in this version, coefficients of determination and slopes included, even though the random forest rows beside them stand.

**What is affected.** Every absolute statistic in prior versions should be treated as withdrawn: all root mean square errors, mean absolute errors, biases, medians, quantiles, mean squared errors, interval widths and threshold counts. Mean squared error and variance columns scale by 2.471 squared, which is 6.106, and interval widths by 2.471. The calibration intercept moves from -0.01121 to -0.02770 while its slope stands.

**Three results are withdrawn outright rather than corrected.** First, the `RD_below_1_only` arm of `national_treemap_headtohead.csv`, in full, including its Pearson coefficients: it selected 20,062 of 21,194 plots by thresholding the defective response at one, and under correction that rule keeps a different sample (retaining 63.6 percent of plots rather than 95.5 percent measured over the full population), so the arm is honestly relabelled as plots with corrected relative density below 2.471, which is no restriction at all; the genuine below one arm must be recomputed and cannot be recovered from the published table. Second, all absolute comparisons against the TreeMap 2022 relative density product of Chivhenge and colleagues, which are cross scale rather than merely rescaled: TreeMap sits at the correct scale, measured at a county median of 0.615, while the quantity beside it was 2.471 times too small; the withdrawn columns are the calibration coefficient of determination, root mean square error, bias and slope in `national_treemap_headtohead.csv` and `maine_treemap_RD_headtohead.csv`, `TreeMap_rmse` in `national_unit_validation.csv`, and `treemap_R2cal` in `sae_benchmark_byscale.csv`; the Pearson coefficients in those tables stand. Third, the three 30 m rasters, which require rebuilding rather than rescaling: they were written with a hard ceiling at 1.5 (`conus_map_postw.py` line 21, `route_a_downscale.R` line 83) stored as relative density times 1000 in 16 bit integers, a ceiling inert on the defective scale that would truncate 20.5 percent of the surface on the corrected one; the ceiling must be lifted before any rescaling.

**Documentation in prior versions asserts the opposite and should not be relied on.** The prior record description, `README.md` at lines 11 to 12 and line 30, and the `data_dictionary.csv` row for `conus_rd_uncapped_by_pid.csv` all describe the response as units consistent. It is not. Seven published scripts carry comments asserting that the response is units consistent or metric, and `ext_disturbance_substitution.R` at its line 29 contains the defective ratio itself, so rerunning it regenerates the error rather than inheriting it. More seriously, `20260622_ThreadB_RD_mapping_memo.md` and `20260623_redteam_stress_test.md` appear to describe this exact defect as a problem that was found and fixed, reporting the two distributions inverted, with the correct distribution presented as the defect and the defective one as the repair. That reading has not been independently confirmed, could not be rendered from the environments used to prepare this correction, and is published here flagged as unconfirmed rather than held back; it will be finalised against the two files whenever they can be read.

**A second, much smaller units issue is noted and not corrected.** The factor 2.471 restores this work to its own stated convention, but that convention omits the diameter reference term. The strictly correct additive stand density index conversion at a 25 cm reference is 2.471054 x (2.54/2.5)^1.605 = 2.534817, so metric stand density index derived from this record remains about 2.5 percent low even after the correction above. This changes no conclusion and is stated for completeness.

**If you hold a downloaded copy of any prior version of this record**, then: multiply the `RD` column of `conus_rd_uncapped_by_pid.csv` by 2.471 before use; multiply every small area estimate, prediction interval bound and interval width by 2.471, and every mean squared error and variance by 6.106; discard any absolute error statistic you computed; retain any Pearson or Spearman correlation you computed on a fixed sample; retain a coefficient of determination or Nash Sutcliffe efficiency only where you refit a random forest on the corrected response, and never where you computed it against the published saved model fit; do not use `conus_mosvr_fit_postw_full.rds` or any of the three 30 m rasters from prior versions without rebuilding them; and cite this erratum alongside the record. Note also that a Canadian file of the same name `conus_rd_uncapped_by_pid.csv` naming convention exists in related work on a different scale; joining the defective file to the Canadian counterpart manufactures an apparent cross border gap of 0.2821 against 0.5306 that is entirely an artefact, whereas the corrected comparison is 0.6970 against 0.5306.
