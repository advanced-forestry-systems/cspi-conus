# Stress test of today's open hypotheses, plus a GRSI feasibility prototype

*25 August 2026, afternoon follow-up to `na-cspi-rd-autopilot-run_FINAL_2026-08-25.md`. Three more Cardinal tracks, run in parallel: the RD30 A1 gap diagnosis, the ESI30 leave-one-province-out transfer test, and a feasibility prototype of Salas-Eljatib (2020) GRSI as a candidate refinement to ESI. Each track's full report is the source of record; this file is the cross-track synthesis.*

## RD30 A1 gap: diagnosed, not closed

The mean-versus-median calibration hypothesis is real but incomplete. `route_a_sae_calibrate.R` does fit a mean-anchored per-hex EBLUP and apply it as a multiplicative ratio, which mechanically preserves the raw map's own mean-to-median skew rather than correcting to the plot median. But the observed 9.9 percent gap is the net of two much larger, partly offsetting defects, not one clean mechanism: a compression pattern (low-RD plots calibrated 2 to 4 times too high, high-RD plots calibrated to roughly half their true value), and a separate, spatially concentrated defect in which 25.5 percent of all 123,173 plots sit on a raw pre-calibration pixel of zero, concentrated in ten low-forest-cover states (Iowa, North Dakota, Nebraska, Utah, South Dakota, Kansas, Arizona, Nevada, Texas, New Mexico), which no multiplicative rescaling can lift. Three candidate fixes were tested against a genuine held-out split; the closest, per-hex quantile matching, still misses tolerance at 0.024 against the 0.02 bar. RD30 CONUS stays PROVISIONAL. Two separable follow-on tracks are needed, refining the quantile match and separately repairing the zero-pixel population in the flagged states, and they should be scheduled as two tracks rather than one.

## ESI30 transfer: holds up

The leave-one-province-out test that BGI30 failed catastrophically on (pooled NSE -0.839, negative in two of three provinces) does not repeat for ESI30. Pooled LOPO R^2 = 0.2852 (calibration slope 1.094, n = 18,454), against a blocked in-domain R^2 of 0.4891 from this morning's gate rerun. Both held-out provinces clear the PASS or EQUIVOCAL band, Quebec at R^2 = 0.3110 (PASS) and New Brunswick at 0.1960 (EQUIVOCAL), neither negative. The one real caveat is that the test only had two provinces to work with (Quebec and New Brunswick), a narrower design than BGI30's three-province test even though the result is stronger. ESI30 is now the first of the four originally requested 30 m layers to clear both an in-domain gate and a genuine transfer test on the same day. The working interpretation is that a directly observable canopy-height target transfers better through an embeddings model than a multi-decadal growth rate inferred from one year of embeddings, which is consistent with why BGI30 struggled in the first place.

## GRSI feasibility: conditional go, not yet defensible

Christian Salas's growth-rate site index reframes site quality as height growth rate at a reference height rather than height at a reference age, an autonomous differential equation in height that needs a measured height increment over a known remeasurement interval, not calendar age since stand establishment. That property is exactly what the project's Canada work needs, since the current ESI response depends on anamorphic Chapman-Richards projection to a common base age, the same mechanism already implicated once in the 14 June SICOND-ESI base-age correction and flagged as fragile for naturally regenerated, uneven-aged boreal stands in the Canada extension scoping.

The prototype found a real, large, field-measured height-remeasurement panel on Cardinal, the Canadian MAG plot network, not FIA as first assumed, since FIA's TREE_GRM tables were not confirmed this session to carry paired field height. Fitting Salas's Eq 12-13 (nonlinear mixed effects, random effects on both alpha and beta, per species) on that panel converged for only 12 of 41 qualifying species, and two of the twelve returned biologically indefensible asymptotes, for example a *Thuja plicata* alpha of 11.55 m against a species that routinely exceeds 40 to 60 m. The correlation between the resulting GRSI and the existing ESI response, over the 12 converged species pooled, is essentially zero (Pearson r = -0.036, Spearman rho = -0.122, n = 12,514), which is genuinely ambiguous rather than a clean negative result, since a null correlation is equally consistent with GRSI carrying real orthogonal information or with the convergence and realism problems corrupting the GRSI side. The decisive test, whether GRSI closes the CONUS-Canada transfer gap that plain ESI has (within-CONUS R^2 0.785, transfer to Canada 0.122), could not be run, since no CONUS field-height-pair source was confirmed.

Recommendation: conditional go. The narrow, identifiable next step is refitting with a single random effect on beta only (the paper's own baseline model, dropping the joint alpha-beta random specification that is producing the non-convergence and the unrealistic asymptotes), confirming whether FIA TREE_GRM actually carries paired field height so the CONUS side becomes possible, and only then running the three-way transfer comparison that would actually answer whether GRSI is worth building into the pipeline. Nothing here should be read as GRSI already working; it is read as GRSI being worth the next, better-specified attempt. No existing ESI script or product was touched; the prototype lives in new, clearly separate files.

## What this changes about the Zenodo Option 1 versus Option 2 decision

The RD30 diagnosis strengthens the case for Option 2 (notice-first). The gap is now understood well enough to know it needs two more distinct repair tracks, not a quick recalibration, so tying the correction's publication to "the rebuild finishes" (Option 1) has an open-ended timeline. Option 2 gets the units-defect warning to the 336 existing downloaders now and ships the rebuilt rasters in a later 1.5.0 once both RD30 repair tracks land. This is a recommendation, not a decision made on Aaron's behalf.
